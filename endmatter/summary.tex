\chapter{Summary}

In this thesis we investigated topics of improving FAIRness, accessibility, and reducing barriers [TODO: barrier to what? analyzing big data?].
We presented the results of this research in pairs of scientific and technical publications, across several scales of investigation; from exploration of simple prokaryotes in a classroom (\cref{chapter:annotation}), through analysis of human cells in a clinical setting (\cref{chapter:clinic}), to wider applications and dissemination in global context (\cref{chapter:training}).

In \cref{chapter:annotation} we presented a working implementation of the GMOD suite of genomics infrastructure for the application of teaching undergraduate bacteriophage genomics courses.
We used over 50 new or updated tools across three workflows to annotate more than 160+ phages to date over several years of testing in undergraduate environments.
Additionally, we presented an implementation of the Circos plotting tool which significantly improves accessibility and discoverability of Circos and eases its use in reproducible scientific workflow.
In \cref{chapter:clinic} we built upon this foundation for the analysis of RNA sequencing data collected from endothelial cells and pericytes exposed to TNF.
We discovered that TNF has a significant impact on both endothelial cells and pericytes, and that pericytes, when exposed to TNF, have a significant impact on endothelial cell permeability with implications for optimising drug delivery in tumours.
In the technical publication of this chapter we explored securing access to sensitive clinical datasets and removing barriers for researchers by providing a more integrated and user-friendly access method.
Finally, in \Cref{chapter:training} we looked to dissemination of these important results through training, how best to ensure sustainability for newly developed tools and resources.
We presented a series of improvements to the Galaxy Training Network to improve FAIRness, increase accessibility, and remove barriers to access.
We presented a number of improvements which directly impact course didactics and improve teachers' experiences such as automatically generated Jupyter notebook for programming lessons and significantly overhauled accessibility via a thorough screenreader testing program.
The presented small improvements have big impacts, affecting 45-50k visitors per month.
Additionally the process of teaching bioinformatics was simplified through the development of Training Infrastructure as a Service (TIaaS) which has provided training infrastructure for 25k students over 5 years.
