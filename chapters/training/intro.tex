With the wide reaching platform we've built, something that scales globally, we need to do something with it, we need to get it in the hands of actual scientists and researchers and educators to make it useful. You can have the most powerful computer in the world, but if it's sitting unused then what was the point in making it.

But education is a mostly solved problem, save for the scaling. Here we improved the abilities of smaller educators to scale their educational content and resources and put them in front of thousands of budding bioinformaticians! First we discuss how Galaxy works as a fantastic, scalable teaching platform from which to build our base. Next we look into scaling the labour that educators do, especially constantly re-recording of lectures whenever teaching materials are updated. And lastly we discuss how this was put into practice in the Smörgåsbord series of events where all of this technology was showcased for 1600, and 3300 registrants in the first two events of the series.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\ref{chapter:training}.\arabic*]
\itemsep-0.5em
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item \textbf{Fostering accessible online education using Galaxy as an e-learning platform} In this work we discuss how to optimally use Galaxy as an e-learning platform, especially in the context of a global pandemic. I was responsible for sharing one of the user-stories that forms the paper, showcasing how to most effectively use the platform for thousand plus user, global, asynchronous training events. This user story was based upon what we learnt during the first Smörgåsbord event where we could easily host 1600 registered trainees for a week long workshop.
\item \textbf{FANCIFUL (DRAFT)} Here I designed and implemented automatic generation of videos from slides and course materials based on human annotated markup.
\item \textbf{SOLSTICE (DRAFT)} Here I co-led the Smörgåsbord events with Saskia Hiltemann. Together we built a training event and video library platform, and wrote automation code around this to make it easier to reuse. We also discuss the Smörgåsbord events and how amazingly successful they are.

\end{enumerate}
