Genome annotation is the cornerstone on which all modern genomics and bioinformatics research is built. Just DNA or RNA by itself is more or less meaningless for research, especially clinical research if we cannot tell which genes there are to be up- or down-regulated. Manual annotation is extremely important for high quality results, as automatic annotation in genomics experiences a significant ``garbage in garbage out'' problem, as well as ``annotation drift'' where propagation of annotation is not always correct.

This chapter presents a series of tools centered around the Generic Model
Organism Database (GMOD) community, a set of independent projects which all
interoperate to provide a scalable platform for genome annotation, from sequencing to publication.

We discuss first how to build a collaborative manual genome annotation platform which enables scaling of the limited number of human annotators and training of the next generation of annotators.
The platform is then expanded and integrated with ontology toolkits ensuring best practice FAIR community databases.
%Lastly, we make the infrastructure itself re-usable via containerisation and share that with the world, such that other deployments can re-create our designs.
The combined power of the GMOD platform is then leveraged to enable an entire curriculum and phage annotation pipeline around these systems and interconnections.

This chapter contains the following sub-chapters:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\ref{chapter:annotation}.\arabic*]
\itemsep-0.5em
\setcounter{enumi}{-1}
\item \textbf{Apollo: Democratizing genome annotation.} In this work we present the new Apollo platform, rewritten from the ground to provide a scalable web interface which enables novice to expert annotators to work together and collaborative annotate genomes with a ``Google Docs'' like experience. I contributed to Apollo over the years in my role as a Phage annotator and Apollo deployer, as well as in the interconnection with Galaxy and enabling new high-throughput annotation techniques.

\item \textbf{Tripal v3: an ontology-based toolkit for construction of FAIR biological community databases.} In this work we discuss the Tripal platform which extends Chado into a fully featured FAIR analysis frontend leveraging JSON-LD for machine interoperability. Here I worked on the deployment of Tripal via Docker containers which allow for easy re-usable deployments that interoperate with other GMOD projects.

% \item \textbf{Galaxy Genome Annotation Platform.} I conceived of the galaxy genome annotation project and imported a lot of my Galaxy tools that I'd written (Python, XML.) Together with Anthony Bretaudeau (INRAE) we further developed this framework and added numerous Docker containers which allow for small laboratories to setup their own Galaxy-Genome Annotation deployment, and start annotating a genome straight away.

\item \textbf{Galaxy and Apollo as a biologist-friendly interface for high-quality cooperative phage genome annotation.} In this work we discuss the development of Galaxy and Apollo together to form a highly reproducible and interoperable annotation workbench that's accessible even to undergraduate students. I started the work (Python, XML, Docker) to make Galaxy and Apollo interoperable by developing numerous tools and bridges between the two services, before working together with other members of the Center for Phage Technology to scale this up into a production service beyond the needs of their phage annotation course.

\end{enumerate}
