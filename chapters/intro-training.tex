The previous chapters covered building a platform, expanding it for genome annotation, and leveraging it for translational research. This platform and these uses are fantastic improvements in isolation, however if a tree falls in the forest, does anyone hear it? If a tool or paper is published, does anyone learn the analysis is available?

Thus we change focus to training and dissemination of results. We improve the existing training infrastructure to improve accessibilities and decrease barriers in the Galaxy Training paper, before looking at the infrastructure's use as an e-learning platform when SARS-CoV-2 forced everyone to isolate. This platform is then scaled to 7,5k learners in the Smörgåsbord series of events, bringing analyses mentioned in this thesis, amongst others, to the world.

Here we improved the abilities of smaller educators to scale their educational content and resources and put them in front of thousands of budding bioinformaticians! First we discuss how Galaxy works as a fantastic, scalable teaching platform from which to build our base. Next we look into scaling the labour that educators do, especially constantly re-recording of lectures whenever teaching materials are updated. And lastly we discuss how this was put into practice in the Smörgåsbord series of events where all of this technology was showcased for 1600, and 3300 registrants in the first two events of the series.

\item \textbf{Galaxy Training: A Powerful Framework for Teaching!} Here we discuss updates to the Galaxy Training Network project which has seen significant changes since it's initial publication. The work was started by Saskia Hiltemann (Jekyll, Ruby, Markdown, HTML, Bash) and Bérénice Batut, and development continued with the addition of myself. I worked to enhance the accessibility of the application--as a part-time screenreader user I was familiar with common failure modes--improving the WCAG compliance as well as use of ARIA roles throughout. Additionally I implemented new plugins for the GTN Jekyll deployment improving build efficiency and reducing necessary computation time and hopefully energy and emission usage. I implemented a slide deck to video converter directly in the GTN allowing trainers to annotate slide decks and produce 33 (as of this writing) constantly updated videos that are automatically generated whenever slides change. Additionally I contributed a number of coding tutorials teaching students introductory Python and R. I rewrote our JSON-LD implementation, a system by which machines can read and understand the content represented on a webpage, and coordinated the integration with BioSchemas's release of a Training schema, and the automatic ingestion of our materials into TeSS (Training eSupport System).

\item \textbf{Training Infrastructure as a Service} I developed TIaaS, a web-based application to enable educators to request access to dedicated computation resources for their courses in connection with Galaxy. I subsequently coordinated the deployment of this service across all UseGalaxy.* instances, which provided computation power for the Smörgåsbord series of events which recorded 7,5k registrations over three years.


\end{enumerate}
