\section{In the Cell}

In this thesis a scalable, real-time collaborative, FAIR platform for genome annotation was presented that meets the needs of both educational as well as research contexts.
\cref{chapter:phages} leveraged existing, open source, community maintained software components (Apollo, Galaxy) which both exist under the Generic Model Organism Database (GMOD) umbrella and provided a series of tools which enhances their integration and interoperability.
This was examined through the lens of phage annotation and education; a suite of tools was produced via novel developments and integration of existing open source bioinformatics tools, and workflows constructed which aggregated all of those tools to enable the structural and functional annotation of bacteriophages.
This unified interface to annotation enabled the publication of over 160%\footnote{\url{https://scholar.google.com/scholar?start=40&q=\%7Bhttps://cpt.tamu.edu/galaxy-pub/+complete+genome+sequence}}
complete, annotated genome sequences of phages since going into operation, supporting all steps of the process from sequencing to GenBank submission preparation.

Additionally we investigated making the production of publication quality plots more accessible via the development of the Galactic Circos wrapper in \cref{chapter:circos} which significantly simplified the user experience of leveraging Circos for complex multi-dimensional data visualisation.
Circos presented an accessibility barrier in terms of the time required to become proficient in its operation due the complex interface it supports, the simplified interface removes that barrier allowing more genome annotators and genome visualisers to build complex plots as a part of their genome annotation workflows.

\section{Out in the World}

\Cref{chapter:gtn} shows the multi-year set of improvements to the Galaxy Training Network, a well used FAIR training platform to ensure that it meets accessibility best practices to ensure content distributed via this channel is accessible to those with visual or hearing impairments, that provides a framework for educators to collaboratively manage peer-reviewed learning materials.
During this development cycle the platform added coding interactive tutorials available as automatically generated Jupyter notebooks, automatic video generation, a comprehensive linting framework, and more.
All features which aim to reduce the barriers to entry and burden of maintenance on educators and those wishing to disseminate their results.
Simultaneously this framework explored improvements the accessibility of the content, in all senses, for learners by providing a freely accessible, learning platform that integrates well with Galaxy.
The application thereof was explored via the implementation of an open source training infrastructure sharing platform \cref{chapter:tiaas} which provided a method for system administrators to offer training infrastructure for educators. This saw 24k learners use the platform over its 5 years of existence, approximately 75\% of which used materials hosted on the aforementioned GTN. The reduction of barriers in educators providing courses was deeply felt based on the feedback received, the complexity of managing infrastructure was previously one of the most painful points of organising an event, completely removed leveraging the combination of Galaxy and TIaaS and the GTN.

\section{Conclusion}

In this thesis multiple themes in the world of Bioinformatics were explored, from the annotation of a single organism, through their exploration in the clinic, and finally to the dissemination of the knowledge obtained throughout the first two arcs. We applied the FAIR and Open Source Software and Open Science principles throughout the development process to ensure the quality and interoperability of our results.

This work has already impacted an incredibly large number of individuals around the world, multiple orders of magnitude more than the typical bioinformatics software release. More focus should be given to these surrounding aspects of the bioinformatics process, rather than simply tool development, as they can provide a force multiplier for the tool development itself. Building a dissemination and training platform, and providing training infrastructure, has ensured that tools and software that would be otherwise unknown, are not only available to, but intelligible and usable to a global audience.
